About us

Who are we?

Mental Ascend was founded by two sports mental coaches with different backgrounds and one shared goal: helping athletes become mentally prepared, self-aware and ready to perform under pressure.

Zoltán Stieber

Co-founder · Mentor · Sports mental coach

With 20 years in professional sport, Zoltán represented Hungary 26 times and played in the Bundesliga, MLS and UEFA Euro 2016, where he scored against Austria. He understands the mental demands of competing abroad, managing injuries and performing under pressure. He combines elite-sport experience with practical mentoring to help athletes take their next step through difficult times and important decisions.

Zsófia Boros

Co-founder · ICF ACC coach · Sports mental & team coach

After 13 years of competitive handball, Zsófia spent a decade in international business leadership, including as Central European Marketing Director at Procter & Gamble, where high performance, responsibility and pressure were part of everyday life. As an ICF ACC credentialed coach, she combines this experience with structured sports mental and business coaching to help athletes, parents and teams build focus and strong mental foundations.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Mental Ascend for?

We primarily support young athletes and elite performers who want to compete with greater mental readiness and stability. Some services are also available to teams, clubs and coaches.

What can you help me with?

Our work may focus on confidence, concentration, motivation, competition pressure, recovering from mistakes or managing performance anxiety. The aim is to help you perform with greater awareness and stability.

Do I need to have a serious problem to benefit from coaching?

No. Coaching can also help you build confidence, manage pressure more effectively or prepare more intentionally for upcoming challenges.

What is the difference between coaching, mentoring and sports psychology?

Coaching uses questions and exercises to help you find your own goals and solutions. Mentoring can also draw on relevant first-hand sporting experience and practical perspectives. For deeper difficulties or significant competition anxiety, support from a sports psychologist may be more suitable.

How are parents involved?

A parent's role can be important, especially when the athlete is under 18, so we agree on the boundaries together at the start. The athlete's own goals and motivation remain central to the work.

Are conversations confidential?

We treat personal information shared in sessions confidentially. For under-18 athletes, we agree in advance what general feedback may be shared with a parent.

Are sessions online or in person?

Our programmes are primarily online, making it easier to fit sessions around training, competitions and study. In-person meetings may also be possible by arrangement.

How many sessions will I need?

That depends on your goals and current situation. A specific challenge may be addressed over a few sessions, while longer-term development can take several months.

Do you work with teams, coaches and clubs?

Yes. We can tailor team sessions, workshops and consultations to your needs, including mental preparation, communication, cohesion and performing under pressure.

What if the athlete is not ready to ask for help?

Coaching works best when the athlete experiences the process, at least partly, as their own choice. A parent can raise the possibility without forcing it; a free consultation lets the athlete learn about the process with no obligation.

How do I know which kind of support is right for me?

You do not need to decide in advance whether you need coaching, mentoring or another kind of support. Book a free 20-minute consultation and we will discuss your goals and help identify a suitable option.

What happens during the free 20-minute consultation?

We learn about your situation, discuss your goals and explore what kind of support might suit you. The consultation does not commit you to buying a programme.
